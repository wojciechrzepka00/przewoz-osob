Organizing a School Trip by Bus — What a Teacher-Guardian Must Remember

25.10.2026

Every school year, dozens of classes from Kraków and the surrounding region set off for Wieliczka Salt Mine, Ojców National Park, Energylandia in Zator, Auschwitz-Birkenau, or the mountains near Zakopane. As a local carrier operating daily on these exact routes, we see the same organizational mistakes repeated — and the same simple fixes that make a trip go smoothly. If you're a teacher or guardian planning a school outing with a hired minibus or coach, here's what actually matters.

Before the trip: paperwork and headcount

Polish regulations require a passenger list with names, and for trips outside the school's home town, written parental consent for each child. Prepare this list in advance and hand a copy to the driver — in case of any inspection on the A4 or S7, this saves everyone time. Also confirm the exact number of participants early, because it determines whether you need an 8-seat bus (ideal for a small group plus two guardians, e.g., a short trip to the Wieliczka Salt Mine or Kraków's Błonia for an outdoor lesson), a 20-seat bus (the most common choice for a single school class), or a full-size coach for around 50 people (typical for grade-wide trips to Zakopane or the Tatra foothills).

Remember the legal minimum: one guardian per roughly 15 children for younger pupils, and always at least two adults per group regardless of size, in case one needs to stay with a child who feels unwell. Make sure every guardian has a phone number for the driver and vice versa — our drivers always exchange contacts with the lead teacher before departure, which matters especially on multi-stop itineraries like Kraków–Ojców–Pieskowa Skała.

Check the vehicle has valid seatbelts for every seat and that the driver is briefed on planned stops. If the itinerary includes a mountain route to Zakopane or a longer ride to Auschwitz-Birkenau (around 1.5–2 hours from Kraków), build in a short break — most groups stop at a MOP (motorway service area) along the S7 or A4, where there are toilets and a place to eat packed lunches.

On the day: boarding, the road, and return

Arrange pickup directly at the school entrance or a nearby safe spot with easy bus access — in central Kraków, streets like Rondo Mogilskie or al. Pokoju can be congested, so for schools in the Old Town or Kazimierz it's often faster and safer to agree on a short walk to a quieter loading point. Do a headcount before departure, after every stop, and before leaving the final destination — this is the single most important habit for any guardian, and experienced drivers will remind you, but the responsibility is yours.

Plan toilet and stretch breaks roughly every 1.5–2 hours, especially with younger children.
