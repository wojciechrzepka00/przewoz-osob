Organizing a School Trip by Bus — What Every Guardian Must Remember

31.10.2026

Spring and early autumn are peak season for school trips around Kraków and Małopolska — a day at the Wieliczka Salt Mine, a hike in Ojców National Park, a visit to Auschwitz-Birkenau, a ride to Energylandia in Zator, or a longer outing to Zakopane and the Tatras. As a local carrier that works with Kraków schools every week, we see the same questions from teachers and guardians over and over. Here's what actually matters when you're planning transport for a class of students.

Legal requirements and guardian ratios

Before you even think about the bus, sort out the paperwork. Polish regulations require written parental consent for every participant, a complete list of students with PESEL numbers, and at least one qualified first-aid kit on board (which our vehicles carry as standard). The guardian-to-student ratio depends on the trip: for excursions within Kraków itself, one adult per 30 students is usually enough, but for anything outside the city — Wieliczka, Niepołomice, Zakopane, Auschwitz — you need one guardian per 15 students, and for multi-day trips this ratio tightens further. Schools organizing trips to Zator or the Tatras often forget this until the last week, so check it early with your school's trip coordinator.

It's also worth confirming NNW accident insurance for the group before departure — most schools arrange this automatically, but double-check if you're a class representative organizing the outing yourself rather than going through the school office.

Choosing the right vehicle and route

Group size decides everything. For a single class of up to 20 kids plus guardians, our 20-seat minibus is the practical choice — easier to park near attractions like the Wieliczka mine entrance or the gates of Ojców, and more fuel-efficient on short regional hops. For whole-grade trips of 40–50 students, we recommend a full coach, which also gives more luggage space — important if you're heading to the mountains and kids are bringing extra layers, or going to Energylandia with bags for the day.

Pickup logistics matter more than people expect. In Kraków, loading 30 children directly in front of a school on a narrow street during morning rush hour (think Grzegórzecka, Dietla, or the area around Rondo Mogilskie) can be genuinely difficult.
