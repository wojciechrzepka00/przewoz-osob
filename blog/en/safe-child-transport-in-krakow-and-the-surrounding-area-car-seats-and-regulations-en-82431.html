Safe Child Transport in Kraków and the Surrounding Area — Car Seats and Regulations

10.11.2026

Whether you're organising a school trip from Nowa Huta to the Wieliczka Salt Mine, booking a transfer for your family to Kraków-Balice airport, or arranging daily transport for children to a sports club in Zabierzów, child safety on the road is not something to leave to chance. At Przewóz Osób Kraków (Legendary Kraków), we deal with this question every week, and we know exactly what Polish law requires — and what parents and school coordinators in Małopolska actually need in practice.

What Polish Law Says About Child Seats

Under Polish traffic regulations, every child under 150 cm in height and under 12 years old must travel in an approved child restraint system suited to their weight and height — whether that's a car seat, a booster seat with a backrest, or a booster cushion for older children. This applies not only to private cars but also to vans and buses used for organised transport, including school outings, workplace shuttles, and wedding guest transfers where children are on board. Drivers and organisers in Kraków are legally responsible for making sure every child passenger is properly secured before the vehicle leaves — not just buckled in generally, but seated in equipment matched to their size.

For larger vehicles, such as our 20-seat buses or coaches used for school trips to places like Ojców National Park, Auschwitz-Birkenau, or Zakopane, the rules are slightly different from a private car, but the principle is the same: children need appropriate restraint and supervision. Many schools in Kraków and nearby towns like Wieliczka, Skawina, or Niepołomice now require written confirmation from the carrier that seating arrangements meet these standards before they'll approve a trip — and we provide that documentation as standard.

How We Handle Child Transport in Practice

Our drivers are used to carrying children on everything from short runs across the city — Krowodrza to Kazimierz, for example — to longer regional trips toward Nowy Targ, Tarnów, or the Pieniny. For families booking airport transfers to Kraków-Balice or Katowice-Pyrzowice, we recommend letting us know in advance if a car seat or booster is needed, including the child's approximate age and weight, so we can prepare the right equipment rather than improvising on the day. The same applies to wedding transport: if guests are bringing young children between the ceremony venue and the reception hall, we simply need a heads-up a day or two beforehand.

For schools and kindergartens organising excursions, we work directly with trip coordinators to plan seating in advance — not just who sits where, but which seats have the right restraint systems fitted, and how many adult chaperones are needed per group under Polish school-trip guidelines. This matters especially on longer routes, such as a day trip from Kraków to the mountains, where children may be seated for two or three hours at a stretch.

For company shuttle routes carrying employees' children — for example, to summer camps or local sports events around Wieliczka or Niepołomice — we also plan pick-up points carefully, choosing safe, well-lit spots with easy parking rather than busy roadside stops, which matters a great deal in Kraków's denser districts like Podgórze or the Old Town periphery during rush hour.
