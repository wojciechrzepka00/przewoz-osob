Organizing a School Trip by Bus — What Every Chaperone Needs to Remember

10.10.2026

Every spring and autumn, Kraków schools organize dozens of trips to places like the Wieliczka Salt Mine, Ojców National Park, the Auschwitz-Birkenau Memorial, or further afield to Zakopane and the Tatra foothills. As a local carrier operating daily on these exact routes, we know which details decide whether a school trip runs smoothly or turns into a logistical headache for the teacher in charge. Here's what actually matters.

Choosing the right vehicle for the group

The size of the group determines everything else. For a single class of up to 20 pupils, an 8- or 20-seat minibus is usually the practical choice — easier to park near schools in tight spots like Kazimierz, Stare Miasto, or Krowodrza, and quicker to load and unload at stops such as the Wieliczka Salt Mine car park or the entrance to Ojców National Park. For two or three classes traveling together, a coach for up to 50 passengers makes more sense financially and logistically, especially on longer routes like Kraków–Zakopane along the Zakopianka, where fewer vehicles mean less convoy coordination and fewer stops. Always confirm the exact headcount — including accompanying teachers — before booking, since Polish regulations require specific seatbelt availability for every child, and we assign vehicles accordingly.

Pickup location matters too. Many Kraków schools don't have space for a coach to pull up directly outside, particularly in the city center or in older buildings in Podgórze and Nowa Huta. We always recommend scouting the pickup point in advance — a nearby street with proper stopping space, or coordinating a short walk from the school entrance to the bus. This avoids delays and keeps the group moving on schedule, which is critical if the itinerary includes a timed entry, like a guided tour at the Salt Mine or Auschwitz-Birkenau, where tour slots are fixed and non-negotiable.

Documentation, supervision ratios, and timing

Before the trip, the school is required to prepare a trip card (karta wycieczki) listing the route, departure and return times, the list of participants, and the assigned chaperones. As the carrier, we need this information too — particularly the final headcount and any planned stops — to plan the route and estimate travel time realistically, factoring in traffic on routes like the A4 toward Tarnów or the often congested Zakopianka during weekends and holiday periods.

Supervision ratios are the teacher's legal responsibility, not the driver's, but a good driver will always help enforce basic safety rules: no standing while the vehicle is moving, seatbelts fastened before departure, and a clear headcount before leaving every stop. We recommend assigning one chaperone to sit near the front and one near the back of the bus, especially with younger children, so no one is overlooked during a toilet or lunch stop.
